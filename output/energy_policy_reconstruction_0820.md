## One-sentence thesis

China's coal-intensive provinces show evidence of operating adjustment, but the coal-power fleet did not exhibit a corresponding acceleration of retirement; this disjuncture is consistent with a transition-constraint bundle involving asset vintages, resource-linked fiscal and employment exposure, reliability needs, and uneven renewable absorption rather than with an automatic conversion of efficiency gains into asset exit.

## Replacement title and abstract

### Title

**Operational Adjustment without Asset Exit: Coal-Power Lock-in and Transition Constraints in China, 2000–2023**

### Abstract (first drafting pass)

Coal transition is often inferred from improvements in energy intensity, emissions, or the coal share of energy use. These measures, however, need not imply the retirement of coal-power assets. This study examines the gap between operating adjustment and asset exit in China using a 31-province panel for 2000–2023, reconstructed coal, wind, and solar project histories from the Global Energy Monitor trackers, and coal-unit retirement records. The evidence distinguishes operating, compositional, and asset-exit margins. Provincial patterns show post-2012 reductions in coal-use intensity and coal's share of final energy in more coal-exposed provinces, although the intensity estimates are sensitive to pre-trends and province-specific trends and are not interpreted as the causal effect of a single policy. In contrast, direct asset evidence shows no statistically detectable acceleration of coal-unit retirement in high-coal-exposure provinces in the current survival specifications. Reconstructed national coal-power operating capacity increased from approximately 686 GW in 2011 to 1,143 GW in 2023, even as wind and solar capacity expanded. Resource-linked fiscal and employment exposure, continuing operating and standby requirements, uneven renewable absorption, and active project pipelines characterize the conditions under which operating adjustment does not become asset exit. A structured comparison of Shanxi and Inner Mongolia illustrates distinct pathways through these constraints. The findings imply that financial and environmental measures directed at coal use cannot substitute for an asset-exit policy package combining retirement finance, replacement capacity, grid integration, and locally tailored fiscal and labour adjustment. The study provides a policy-relevant diagnosis rather than a causal evaluation of green credit or any other individual national intervention.

**Keywords:** coal-power retirement; carbon lock-in; energy transition; transition finance; China; just transition

## Reconstructed article architecture

### 1. Introduction — 900 words

**Job:** Establish the policy puzzle, not a national-policy treatment effect.

1. Start with the mismatch between decarbonizing plant operation and retiring coal assets. Efficiency, pollution controls, and lower coal shares can coexist with long-lived coal capacity.
2. Explain why the distinction matters for climate targets, stranded assets, reliability, and just transition policy.
3. State the research question: *Do observed operating adjustments in coal-intensive Chinese regions coincide with coal-power asset exit? If not, what observable transition constraints characterize the gap?*
4. Introduce the multi-level evidence: 31-province panel, GEM unit and project histories, constraint indicators, and Shanxi–Inner Mongolia comparison.
5. State three contributions:
   - separates operational, compositional, and asset-exit margins;
   - brings actual coal-unit retirement histories into a province-level transition analysis;
   - turns the finding into a targeted policy problem: retirement finance, replacement capacity, local fiscal/employment adjustment, and grid integration must be coordinated.
6. End with the evidence boundary: this is a policy-relevant empirical diagnosis, not the causal evaluation of the Green Credit Guidelines.

### 2. From operating adjustment to asset exit: conceptual framework — 750 words

**Job:** Replace the long green-credit review with a narrow theory of transition constraints.

1. Define three margins:
   - **operating adjustment:** fuel input, utilization, emissions, and efficiency within existing assets;
   - **compositional adjustment:** coal's share of energy use or generation;
   - **asset exit:** retirement, cancellation, mothballing, or permanent repurposing of coal units.
2. Explain why the margins differ in adjustment cost and policy requirements: retirement carries unrecovered capital, reliability, worker, fiscal, and local political costs.
3. Treat financing regulation and green finance as one possible external pressure among environmental regulation, supply-side reform, and climate policy—not the identified driver.
4. Derive the empirical expectation: operating adjustment may occur without exit when transition constraints remain binding.

### 3. Data and empirical strategy — 1,150 words

**Job:** Make the data contribution credible and reproducible.

1. Province-year panel: 31 provinces, 2000–2023; clearly state outcome-specific coverage.
2. GEM lifecycle data: explain that 2026 trackers reconstruct annual start/retirement/stock histories using reported years; call out 30 provinces with coal data, the minimum 30-MW coal-unit scope, and early-history limitations.
3. Coal-unit survival sample: 4,323 units and 952 retirement events; left-truncated, calendar-period Cox model, age/capacity controls, province-clustered uncertainty.
4. Operating outcomes: coal/energy intensity and coal share are supporting outcomes; include the 2012 continuous-exposure analysis only as an association and report the pre-trend/trend sensitivity in the main text.
5. Constraint measures: resource-linked fiscal/employment/asset exposure, coal-unit operating/standby indicators, renewable consumption, utilization hours, monthly source volatility, and policy attention. State the coverage and that these measures are descriptive/contextual unless a model is explicitly identified.
6. Explain the comparison logic: convergence across evidence streams, not a single counterfactual claim.

### 4. Results — 2,500 words

#### 4.1 National asset trajectory: replacement has not become exit

- Show annual national coal additions, retirements, and reconstructed operating stock alongside wind-solar additions/stock.
- Report the central descriptive fact: reconstructed coal stock rises from approximately 686 GW in 2011 to 1,143 GW in 2023, despite substantial wind-solar growth.
- Emphasize that additions and retirements are distinct from changing thermal generation shares.

#### 4.2 Coal-intensive regions: operating adjustment is not matched by accelerated retirement

- Present the Cox results by calendar period and a high/low exposure Kaplan–Meier figure.
- Use precise wording: the current estimates do **not detect** faster retirement in high-coal-exposure provinces after 2012. The early-period contrast is suggestive, not a causal policy break.
- Present provincial lifecycle regressions as corroboration only: no robust differential increase in coal retirement, coal additions, net additions, or coal stock share after 2012.

#### 4.3 The operating margin moved, but its causal interpretation is limited

- Condense the continuous-exposure analysis to coal share and a carefully labelled operating-outcome panel.
- Lead with the result that high coal exposure is associated with larger post-2012 reductions in coal-use composition and selected operating measures.
- Immediately report that intensity and SO2 results have pre-trend or province-trend sensitivity; do not use pollution as a standalone causal result.
- The purpose of this section is to establish the puzzle’s first half, not to attribute it to green credit.

#### 4.4 Constraint profile: why replacement and exit are harder than adjustment

- Organize evidence by constraint rather than by a series of weak interaction regressions:
  1. resource-linked fiscal, employment, and state-asset dependence;
  2. coal-unit operating/standby requirements and generation stability;
  3. renewable consumption, utilization, and curtailment/absorption conditions;
  4. active coal and renewable project pipelines.
- Use a transparent province typology or four-quadrant figure: **high asset lock-in / high renewable absorption**, **high lock-in / low absorption**, etc. Call it a descriptive typology.

#### 4.5 Shanxi and Inner Mongolia: two pathways through the constraint bundle

- Shanxi: coal assets, resource dependence, and a cleaner-use/retrofit orientation.
- Inner Mongolia: large coal assets combined with wind-solar projects and external-power orientation, not automatic coal exit.
- Report policy-text contrasts only for green finance and clean-coal language, as supported by the data. Treat the cases as illustrations of the typology.

### 5. Discussion and policy design — 900 words

1. Explain that operational decarbonization is necessary but cannot be used as a proxy for asset retirement.
2. Match policies to constraints:
   - retirement/repurposing compensation and owner-level transition plans for stranded assets;
   - capacity-replacement, flexibility, storage, and grid absorption policies for reliability;
   - fiscal-transfer, worker-transition, and SOE-governance arrangements for coal-dependent regions;
   - transition-finance disclosure and milestones, rather than green-project screening alone.
3. State the trade-off: a policy that only penalizes coal use may improve operating measures without resolving existing assets.
4. Clarify scope: results do not rank these policies or estimate their effects.

### 6. Conclusion — 250 words

Return to the empirical distinction: operational adjustment occurred alongside persistent coal-power assets. The policy implication is not simply “more green finance,” but a coordinated asset-exit policy package.
