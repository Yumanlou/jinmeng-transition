# Literature map: coal-power adjustment, lock-in, and exit in China

**Purpose.** This is a *dialogue map*, not a generic reference list.  It asks which sources sharpen, qualify, or challenge the manuscript's claim that China experienced operating and compositional transition without an automatic coal-power asset-exit pathway.

**Manuscript being mapped.** `Designed for Adjustment: Coal-Power Lock-in and the Missing Exit Pathway in China, 2000–2023`.

**Core boundary.** The article must not say that a policy was “designed not to retire coal” unless a primary policy text says so.  The defensible claim is narrower: the instruments and observed outcomes in the 2012–2023 window primarily concern cleaner operation, renewable deployment, adequacy, and market/system adjustment; they do not by themselves allocate retirement, replacement, debt, and local-adjustment obligations.  Likewise, the provincial results are observational diagnostics, not an estimate of the Green Credit Guidelines’ causal effect.

## How this corpus was assembled

- **Target size:** 50 sources: 14 sources already cited in the manuscript, 25 new peer-reviewed sources, and 11 primary policy documents.
- **Screening rule:** sources must bear directly on at least one of five claims: lock-in and policy design; coal-plant exit/stranded assets; replacement capacity and grid absorption; finance; or regional fiscal/labour adjustment.  Generic “green innovation” papers were excluded unless they define the green-credit institutional background.
- **Verification:** all new academic titles, journal outlets, years, and DOI records were checked against Crossref (and initially located through OpenAlex) on 2026-08-22.  Existing items were checked against the project BibTeX.  Policy sources are cited by issuing body and official document title; before submission, retrieve and archive the official Chinese text/PDF in `references/policy/`, since official government pages were unavailable to the automated checker in this session.
- **Priority labels:** **A** = cite in the main argument; **B** = targeted support or counterpoint; **C** = policy/background source, normally cited once in the institutional section or an appendix timeline.

## What the map says about the article’s position

### 1. The closest empirical conversation is coal-power phase-out, not generic green-credit evaluation

The most important new citations are **Cui et al. (2019; 2021), Zhang et al. (2022), Heerma van Voss et al. (2022), Yan et al. (2024), Yu et al. (2023), and An et al. (2025)**.  Together, they establish that a credible coal transition requires decisions about retirement timing, cost/risk allocation, system replacement, and political feasibility.  The manuscript adds an observational historical diagnosis: a national transition can show real operating and compositional progress while those exit decisions remain unresolved.

### 2. The paper needs a deliberately two-sided power-system conversation

The optimistic side is **He et al. (2016; 2020), Liu et al. (2019), Davidson et al. (2016), Chen et al. (2022), and Wang et al. (2023)**: China has genuine renewable, storage, transmission and absorption potential.  The constraining side is **An et al. (2025), Zhuo et al. (2022), Shi and Shen (2018), and Gonzalez-Salazar et al. (2018)**: adequacy, flexibility, cost and market design make immediate or undifferentiated closure unsafe.  This balance is crucial.  Without it, “missing exit pathway” can read as an anti-coal slogan; with it, it is a design claim about the conditions for orderly exit.

### 3. The regional paragraph should be converted from a generic justice appeal into a transition-governance claim

Use **Jiang et al. (2023), Diluiso et al. (2021), Green and Gambhir (2020), Hansen and Coenen (2015), and Newell and Mulvaney (2013)** to say that retirement costs are territorially concentrated and therefore require fiscal, labour and regional-development instruments.  The paper’s Figure 3 then becomes a diagnostic typology of where different packages may be needed—not a regression claiming that resource dependence causes delayed retirement.

### 4. Green credit belongs in the institutional background, but should no longer carry the article

Use **CBRC (2012), Aizawa and Yang (2010), Zhang et al. (2011), and Campiglio (2016)** to state what green finance can plausibly do: affect lending conditions, environmental-risk management, retrofit investment and renewable investment.  Then state the boundary: without bank-to-owner/plant lending and debt data, it cannot show that green credit caused either adjustment or the absence of exit.  That is a cleaner, more publishable use of the policy than treating 2012 as a quasi-experiment by default.

## Recommended citation architecture (not yet inserted into the manuscript)

1. **Opening problem:** Unruh (2000); Cui et al. (2019; 2021); Kefford et al. (2018).
2. **Why adjustment differs from exit:** Howlett (2010); Campiglio (2016); Yu et al. (2023); Yan et al. (2024).

4. **Why the provincial dimension matters:** Hansen and Coenen (2015); Jiang et al. (2023); Green and Gambhir (2020).

## Editorial guardrails

- Do not present scenario-model results as confirmation of the manuscript’s historical findings.  Their role is to show what a feasible/just exit pathway would need.
- Do not claim that China lacks transition policy.  The policy documents show a dense policy mix.  The article’s narrower argument is that the mix does not automatically settle the retirement, replacement, finance and local-adjustment package.
- Do not use the 2024 policy document to explain a 2012–2023 coefficient.  It is an update/context source only.
- Before any submission, archive the official policy texts and add page/article pinpoints for every claim about targets, mandates, “three reforms,” capacity pricing, or “orderly transition.”
